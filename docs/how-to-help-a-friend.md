# How to help a friend

You are not their therapist. You *can* be the person who notices, asks, and walks with them to help.

## Before you start

- You can take a break if you are drowning too. Supporting someone does not require self-erasure.
- If they have a plan to die and you are scared, call a crisis line *with* them or emergency services. That is loyalty, not betrayal.
- Keep crisis numbers in your phone: see [crisis-resources.md](crisis-resources.md).

## Opening the conversation

Pick a private-enough moment. Be specific about what you saw.

> "You've seemed checked out since exams started, and you joked about not wanting to wake up. I'm worried. Can we talk?"

> "Are you thinking about suicide?"

If they say yes:

> "Thank you for telling me. You don't have to handle this alone. Can we contact 988 / [local line] together, or tell [trusted adult]?"

If they say no but you are still worried, you can still suggest counseling and keep checking in.

## While they talk

- Let silence exist.
- Reflect: "It sounds like everything feels pointless right now."
- Avoid immediately listing reasons they should be grateful.
- Avoid "but you have so much going for you" as the first sentence.
- Ask what would help *today*: water, sitting together, calling a parent, campus counseling walk-in.

## After

- Text later: "Still with you. Did you get an appointment?"
- Help with the boring logistics (finding the counseling portal, sitting outside the office).
- Do not gossip. Tell an adult if safety requires it; explain to your friend why you did.

## If they tell you not to tell anyone

Confidentiality has a limit when someone may die. You can say:

> "I care about you too much to keep this only between us if you're in danger. I'll go with you to get help."

## Looking after yourself

Secondary stress is real. Use the same crisis lines. Talk to your own trusted adult. You did not cause their pain and you cannot guarantee their outcome. Showing up still matters.
