# Suicide prevention

Suicide is a public health problem and it is **preventable**. This page follows guidance from WHO (LIVE LIFE), CDC, SAMHSA, IASP, and the Suicide Prevention Resource Center. It does **not** describe methods.

**If you are thinking about suicide:** [crisis-resources.md](crisis-resources.md) — call or text your local line now.

## Core message for young people

Having thoughts of suicide does not mean you are weak, dramatic, or doomed. It means you are in more pain than you currently have tools for. Tools and people exist. Many who have had these thoughts later describe them as a chapter, not the whole story.

## Warning signs (from public health agencies)

Watch for clusters, especially if they are new or getting worse:

- Talking about wanting to die, feeling hopeless, or being a burden
- Withdrawing from friends, sports, or family
- Dramatic mood shifts, including a sudden "calm" after severe distress
- Giving away important possessions, saying goodbye
- Increased substance use
- Reckless behavior
- Looking up information about dying (if you see this in a friend, take it seriously and get adult/professional help)
- Sleep collapse or not sleeping at all

Absence of obvious signs does not mean absence of risk. When in doubt, ask and connect.

## How to help someone you are worried about

SAMHSA and 988 guidance:

1. **Ask directly.** "Are you thinking about suicide?" Asking does not plant the idea.
2. **Listen** without arguing them out of their feelings in the first minute.
3. **Stay with them** if it is safe.
4. **Connect** to 988 / local crisis services or a trusted adult and professional care.
5. **Reduce access to lethal means** in the environment when you can do so safely (this is among the most effective individual-level actions in the research literature).
6. **Follow up** the next day. A text matters.

Do **not**:

- Swear to keep an active suicide plan secret
- Leave someone alone who has a plan and intent
- Mock, dare, or debate whether their pain is "valid enough"
- Post graphic details of attempts on social media (this can harm others; follow responsible reporting principles)

Full conversation examples: [how-to-help-a-friend.md](how-to-help-a-friend.md).

## What works at the population level (WHO LIVE LIFE)

WHO's four evidence-based intervention areas:

1. **Limit access to means of suicide** (strongest population evidence)
2. **Interact with media** for responsible reporting (no sensational detail, include help resources, stories of coping)
3. **Foster socio-emotional life skills** in adolescents
4. **Early identification, assessment, management, and follow-up** for anyone affected by suicidal behavior

Supporting pillars: data, multi-sector collaboration, awareness, training, funding, and evaluation.

## What works in care systems

- **Safety planning** (e.g., Stanley-Brown Safety Planning Intervention), endorsed widely, is associated with fewer suicidal behaviors than "no-suicide contracts," which are **not** recommended as a standalone tool.
- **Zero Suicide** framework in health systems: screen, assess, treat, follow up, and support staff.
- After an attempt or crisis visit, **rapid follow-up** reduces risk.
- Treat depression, substance use, and other conditions; treatment of mental disorders is part of prevention, not the whole of it.

CDC's *Suicide Prevention Resource for Action* groups strategies as: strengthen economic supports, create protective environments, improve access to suicide care, promote connections, teach coping skills, identify and support people at risk, and lessen harms after suicidal behavior.

## Schools and campuses

Evidence-aligned activities include:

- Gatekeeper training for staff (recognize, ask, refer)
- Peer-leader programs with adult supervision (e.g., Sources of Strength)
- Mental health literacy that includes how to get help
- Postvention after a death (support survivors, avoid memorial practices that glamorize suicide)

Universal school programs should be implemented with fidelity and never as a one-off assembly that only shows shocking stories.

## If you have lost someone

Grief after suicide is often mixed with shock, anger, and unanswered questions. Specialized survivor resources exist through IASP, local bereavement groups, and 988. You are not responsible for another person's choice; support is still available for *you*.

## Responsible talk and posting

- Include help information when you discuss suicide publicly.
- Avoid describing methods or locations.
- Avoid "committed suicide" if your community prefers "died by suicide" (language varies; respect is the goal).
- Do not share a deceased young person's graphic story as "awareness" without family consent and safety review.

World Suicide Prevention Day is 10 September (IASP / WHO).
