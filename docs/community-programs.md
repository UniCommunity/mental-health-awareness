# Active community and school programs

Programs change. Verify locally before you volunteer time or money. Preference here is for programs that are widely implemented and/or evaluated.

## Peer-led and youth advocacy

### Active Minds
Youth and young-adult network changing campus and high-school culture around mental health. Chapter network, speakers, Send Silence Packing exhibit, Mental Health Advocacy Academy.
https://activeminds.org

### Sources of Strength
Upstream suicide prevention using peer leaders + adult advisors. Focus on eight protective strengths. Multiple evaluations; implemented in thousands of schools.
https://sourcesofstrength.org

### NAMI (National Alliance on Mental Illness)
Ending the Silence presentations; NAMI on Campus clubs; family and peer education; helpline.
https://www.nami.org

### The Trevor Project
Crisis services and education focused on LGBTQ+ young people.
https://www.thetrevorproject.org

## Integrated youth mental health services

### headspace (Australia; model copied internationally)
One-stop centers for ages 12-25: mental health, physical and sexual health, work/school support, alcohol and other drugs. Online service (eheadspace) as well.
https://headspace.org.au

### Youth Mental Health Corps (U.S.)
Near-peer navigators in schools and community sites to help students find care and grow the behavioral health workforce.

## How to start something local without making it worse

1. Partner with existing counseling staff; do not freelance crisis work.
2. Pick one evidence-aligned activity rather than a vague awareness week.
3. Budget for adult supervision and a referral pathway.
4. Include youth in design, especially those most affected.
5. Measure something simple: students who know how to get help; staff trained; wait time to first appointment.
6. Plan postvention before you need it.
