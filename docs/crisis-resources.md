# Crisis resources

**If you are unsafe right now, call local emergency services.**

Crisis lines are for suicidal thoughts, panic, overwhelm, grief, substance crises, and "I just need someone to talk to." You do not need a diagnosis.

## Global directory

- **Find A Helpline** — [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com)  
  Verified phone, text, chat, and WhatsApp lines in 130+ countries. Partnered with the International Association for Suicide Prevention (IASP).
- **Befrienders Worldwide** — [befrienders.org](https://www.befrienders.org)
- **IASP crisis centres** — [iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines](https://www.iasp.info/crisis-centres-helplines/)

## Specialized lines (examples)

- **LGBTQ+ youth:** The Trevor Project (US) — thetrevorproject.org
- **Veterans (US):** 988, then press 1
- **Disaster / community distress:** Many countries have disaster-distress lines; search your public health agency.

## What happens when you call or text

Trained counselors listen, help you slow down, assess safety, and work on next steps. They will not typically send police unless there is imminent danger and no other way to keep someone safe. You can say you are calling for a friend.

## If you are supporting someone in crisis

1. Stay with them if it is safe to do so.
2. Ask directly: "Are you thinking about suicide?"
3. Remove immediate access to lethal means if you can do so safely (this is one of the strongest prevention actions at the individual level).
4. Call 988 / local crisis line together, or emergency services if danger is imminent.
5. Do not promise secrecy about active plans to die.
