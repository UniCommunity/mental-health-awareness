# Best practices

Best practice here means aligned with major public health and clinical guidance, not the only way. Adapt to culture, language, and resources.

## For families and caregivers

- Talk about mental health the way you talk about asthma or a broken bone: matter-of-fact, early, repeatable.
- Notice function: sleep, school attendance, friendships, appetite — not only words.
- If you are worried about suicide, ask the question plainly. Then get professional help.
- Store medications and firearms with serious access barriers. Means safety is a family practice, not an accusation.
- Care for your own mental health. Young people copy regulation more than lectures.
- Partner with the school instead of hoping the problem stays at home.

## For schools and campuses

A useful continuum:

1. **Universal:** belonging, sleep-friendly schedules where possible, SEL / life skills, staff who know how to refer
2. **Targeted:** groups for anxiety, grief, or substance risk
3. **Indicated:** counseling, crisis protocols, warm handoffs to community care
4. **Postvention:** after a death or attempt that becomes known

Do: train staff in recognize-ask-refer; publish how to get help on syllabi; use evaluated programs; include students in design.

Don't: outsource prevention to a single assembly; punish help-seeking as a discipline issue when safety can be handled another way; ignore waitlists.

## For clinicians and clinics (high level)

- Screen only if you have a pathway after a positive screen.
- Prefer safety planning over no-suicide contracts.
- Follow up after emergency or inpatient discharge.
- Involve family when developmentally appropriate and safe.
- Use measurement so you know if treatment is working.
- Address sleep, substances, and social determinants alongside diagnosis.

mhGAP (WHO) guides non-specialist workers in settings with few psychiatrists.

## For community organizations

- Meet young people where they already are.
- Pair activity with optional conversation.
- Partner with people who have lived experience and training/supervision.
- Plan for crisis: staff should know the local number and when to escalate.
- Collect simple feedback from youth.

## For media and student journalists

Follow WHO / IASP media recommendations: include help resources; do not describe methods; avoid sensational headlines; include stories of help-seeking and recovery.

## For policymakers

LIVE LIFE and CDC packages converge on means restriction, funded crisis care, school skills programs, data systems, and economic supports.
