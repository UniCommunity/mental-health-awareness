# For educators, coaches, RAs, and youth workers

You may be the trusted adult a young person actually has.

## Minimum viable protocol

1. Know your organization's crisis procedure on day one.
2. Save 988 / local crisis and child-protection numbers.
3. If a student discloses suicidal thinking, do not leave them isolated. Follow protocol. Notify the designated mental health lead.
4. Document facts, not theories.
5. After a crisis, a warm handoff beats an email into the void.

## Everyday practices that help

- Learn names; greet students. Belonging is a protective factor.
- Put help resources on slides and syllabi as routine, not only after a tragedy.
- Watch for attendance collapse, sudden grade drops, giving away items, or a student who used to talk going silent.
- Do not process another student's suicide in class as true-crime discussion.
- Model repair: "I was sharp yesterday. That wasn't fair."

## Teaching about mental health

Prefer multi-session, skills-based curricula over one assembly. Combine facts with "here is how you get help *in this building*." If you use lived-experience speakers, brief them on responsible language and have counselors present.

NAMI Ending the Silence, Active Minds high-school curriculum, and Sources of Strength are common U.S. options. Check your district's approved list.

## After a suicide in the community (postvention)

Goals: support the grieving, reduce contagion risk, restore routine.

- Coordinate messaging with administration and a crisis team.
- Identify students who were close to the person or who are already vulnerable; reach out.
- Avoid permanent glorifying memorials in hallways; living memorials (service, scholarships, policy change) are safer.
- Give staff their own support. They are not fine just because the bell rang.

Consult SPRC postvention guidance and your public health department.

## Boundaries

You can care without becoming a 24/7 hotline. Tell students when you are available and where after-hours help lives. Refer early. Burned-out adults help fewer young people.
