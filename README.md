# Mental Health Awareness Hub

**Education, prevention, research, and community action for teenagers and young adults.**

> **If you are in crisis or thinking about suicide, get help now.**  
> You do not have to wait until things feel "bad enough." Support is available 24/7.

---

## Get help immediately

| Where | How |
| --- | --- |
| **United States & Canada** | Call or text **988** (Suicide & Crisis Lifeline). Chat: [988lifeline.org](https://988lifeline.org) |
| **Crisis Text Line (US)** | Text **HOME** to **741741** |
| **LGBTQ+ youth (US)** | The Trevor Project: 1-866-488-7386 or [thetrevorproject.org](https://www.thetrevorproject.org) |
| **International** | Find a local helpline: [findahelpline.com](https://findahelpline.com) (IASP partner directory, 130+ countries) |
| **Immediate danger** | Call your local emergency number (911, 112, 000, etc.) |

This repository is **not a substitute for professional care**. It is an educational and awareness project that points people toward evidence-based help.

---

## What this repository is

An open, community-maintained collection of:

- Mental health **education** written for teens and young adults
- **Suicide prevention** guidance aligned with WHO, IASP, CDC, SAMHSA, and NIMH
- Summaries of **research and best practices**
- Practical **advice** commonly shared by clinicians (psychoeducation, not personalized treatment)
- Pointers to **active community and school programs**
- How to support a friend, and how communities can take action

Language is intended to be clear, non-stigmatizing, and honest. Mental health conditions are common, treatable, and not a personal failure.

## Guiding principles

1. **Safety first.** Crisis resources appear before education. This project never describes methods of self-harm.
2. **Evidence over slogans.** Claims should be traceable to WHO, CDC, NIMH, SAMHSA, IASP, peer-reviewed reviews, or named programs with published evaluation.
3. **Ask, listen, connect.** Asking someone if they are thinking about suicide does **not** plant the idea. It can open a path to help. (SAMHSA)
4. **Hope is realistic.** Many people who have been in severe distress recover and live full lives with support.
5. **Culture and context matter.** Programs work best when adapted with the people they serve.

## How to use this repo

- **If you are a young person:** start with education and "how to help a friend." Bookmark crisis numbers.
- **If you are a parent or caregiver:** read professional advice and best practices; talk earlier than you think you should.
- **If you are a teacher, RA, coach, or youth worker:** use the educator guide and program list.
- **If you want to contribute:** see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md). Keep tone calm, cite sources, and never add how-to content about harm.
